# Negotiating Update 25-08
 
Our committee acknowledges the tremendous loss we experienced with the recent passing of Mr. Smith. He was a visionary who changed the way the world works. Without him, none of us would find our lives at this juncture. We honor his memory, spirit, and tenacity. Thank you, Mr. Smith.
 
The NMB ended this week's bargaining sessions early due to a lack of significant progress by our committee. We are disappointed in the Board’s decision, but we respect it. Undoubtedly, we are responsible for our bargaining approach and the associated consequences. As your representatives, our daunting task requires us to represent you, while keeping the mediation process going forward. That can be a tremendous challenge as these recent events proved.
 
The NMB instructed us beforehand to make significant moves during this bargaining round. We took that to heart and prepared diligently, making tough, thoughtful decisions. As a result, we volunteered to go first on Tuesday morning with Sections 3 and 28, although the mediator decided to go forward with a slightly different order. According to ALPA's Economic and Financial Analysis (E&FA) Department and our Retirement consultant, our proposals accounted for an approximate $900 million cost reduction from our most recent previous positions. We honestly thought that taking almost a billion dollars off the table from our most recent proposals at the outset of this week’s bargaining would meet the definition of significant progress. Unfortunately, it did not. As an aside, to date, we have rescinded $1.4 billion in costs associated proposed contractual improvements from our Committee’s original proposals.
 
These latest events represent a microcosm of the difficulties we face. The TA failed. The Company does not want to set a precedent by rewarding what they perceive as our bad behavior for possessing the temerity to reject the ‘23 TA. Even though the Company clearly articulated to the NMB in their response to ALPA's proffer of an Arbitration request that they did not draw a line in the sand regarding adding additional money, their refusal to do so thus far demonstrates otherwise. E&FA confirms that the Company has not brought any new significant value forward. Their pay proposal comes at the expense of Block over 8/10 and an adjusted pay slope of the FO/CA pay ratio. Their retirement proposal is less than the TA version due to the singular FAE cap, which does not step up to $338K.
 
Yes, we lowered our pay rate by one dollar to DL/UA/AA + $6 / hour. That represents a diminutive cost reduction. However, we also abandoned our effort to improve the Trip Rig, resulting in approximately $400 million in cost savings. Without question, our hourly rate proposal did not sit well with the NMB and the Company. However, we all know which pages almost every pilot turns to when a new TA hits the street - the pay tables. Those pages represent a "V1" decision point regarding the overall worth of the TA. Failure to achieve expectations there generally results in an unratifiable TA. In our estimation, the magnitude of hourly rate reduction that the other parties were apparently looking for would have jeopardized any TA.
 
We knew this would be problematic territory, so we attempted to strike a balance between cost reduction and preserving our hourly rate position. 
 
Another fundamental issue where we find no common ground with the Company is in the costing of proposals. The Company maintains that we are still approximately 700 pilots overstaffed. We confirmed that on Tuesday. That's their story, and they're sticking to it. Yet, they insist on costing proposals that project out into the future at the failed TA staffing level. Why would they do that? Doesn't that invalidate 4A2b/c? Costing those departed 300+ pilots certainly inflates every one of our proposals. We maintain that both conditions cannot simultaneously exist, and the costing should accurately reflect the true pilot count. The Company is either 700 pilots overstaffed or not. We all know that answer. Their explanation of using that methodology to reference costs associated with the failed TA for ease of comparison does not hold water. 
 
Regarding the Company's NC Update, in their zeal and rush to control the narrative, the Company failed to mention our significant cost reductions in our proposals while also failing to mention that they maintained their current CBA provisions on 4A2b/c, revisions, and extensions, along with the TA version of the Scope SPR and other items. The Company wasted no time yesterday. The final words from the NMB barely finished echoing off the walls when one of our pilots forwarded us a text that stated we had been "iced." That speedy narrative confirmed a suspected communication pathway for us. All of that ties into their efforts to discredit us. They even sank to a new ultra-low with their last line in the missive, when they walked a lawyerly fine line that described our bargaining actions relative to good and bad faith. We don’t believe that bargaining in a manner necessary to protect your interests or our refusal to acquiesce to what amounts to a reconfiguration of the rejected TA meets either the legal or practical definition of bad faith. 
 
Where are we going from here? We heard, and will comply with the NMB’s instructions. Our committee extensively debriefed the MEC yesterday afternoon on those instructions and other items. They are going to contemplate our debrief. We'll collaboratively form a plan and execute it. The Board left it open for us to contact them as appropriate when ready to reengage. That’s not a definition of being “iced” or “parked.” Our committee understands that’s not what you were hoping to hear at the end of this week’s bargaining, but we are obliged to be forthright on where we stand. We completely understand your frustration and disappointment. We share it. We’re moving forward. That's all we can do. Hang in there, and we'll be in touch.
